For Employers — The Real Questions

Most hesitation around international candidates comes from uncertainty. Here are the direct answers to what you're actually thinking.

"Do we need to sponsor a visa to hire you?"
No — not for the first 3 years. Your company does not file any immigration paperwork, pay any legal fees, or take on any visa risk for the initial work authorization period. I'm as straightforward to hire as any domestic candidate during this time.
"What if we want to keep you after 3 years?"
That's when we'd talk about H-1B sponsorship — which is a routine process for tech companies. The H-1B cap lottery is filed in April, effective October. Thousands of US tech companies do this every year for candidates worth keeping. If you've worked with me for 2–3 years and want to continue, this is a standard business decision, not a complicated one.
"When can you actually start work?"
July 1, 2026. Graduation is May 10. Work authorization activates July 1. If your start date is July 1 or later, there is zero gap or complication.
"Is there any ongoing compliance requirement?"
For the initial 12-month period: none beyond standard hiring paperwork. For the STEM extension (years 2–3): your company needs to be E-Verify enrolled, which is free, takes under a day to set up, and is required for any federal contractor anyway. If you're already E-Verify enrolled, there's literally nothing extra to do.
